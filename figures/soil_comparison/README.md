Melt-out is an operational daily estimate, not the precise first zero-snow hour. It requires seven consecutive snow-free days (daily max depth <=1 cm and, for models, max SWE <=1 kg/m2), after the last snow-covered day from the seasonal peak through August 31. Daily validity requires 18 hourly values, a seasonal peak >=10 cm, and >=90% valid March–August days. Missing observation coverage produces missing melt dates and explicit statuses. No observation gaps are filled. Observed melt dates use depth only. Exact timestamp joins prevent the extra initial cold-start hour in some base runs from shifting comparisons. Final years may be partial September, matching existing base endpoints.

These idealized soil runs move modeled melt-out earlier in all tested forcing-seasons: PPSA 4–9 days, TRAM 2–10 days, HEEN 3–11 days. Hourly depth RMSE improves in 8/12, 6/9 and 7/9 forcing-seasons respectively. These are comparisons on available observation hours, not independent statistical samples or proof that the assumed soil is accurate. First forcing-pair seasons are initialization-sensitive; post-first-season summaries are separate. Soil changes matter for melt timing, but the observation agreement does not improve uniformly. Base imposed TSG is replaced by a calculated soil column and geothermal lower boundary, so that boundary change is part of this sensitivity experiment.

The mixed HEEN forcing produces large-snowfall warnings on 2021-02-09 and 2022-02-01; the unchanged-input results and visible spikes are retained. See per-site run logs and verification JSONs. No other sites, forcing versions, original inputs or prior outputs were modified.
